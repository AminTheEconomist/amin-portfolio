Validation — how I know it's right

The source workbook (ginzu_aapl.xlsb, 19 sheets + VBA) holds Apple's FY2025 numbers. Its 'Valuation output'!B33 is $109.874036. The engine on this page is the exact file that a Node test harness ran against a dump of that sheet — 177 cells (growth, revenue, margin, EBIT, tax, EBIT(1−t), reinvestment, FCFF, NOL, WACC, discount factors, PVs, invested capital, ROIC, terminal value, equity bridge) — with a maximum relative difference of 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ (harness run 2026-09-09). The Monte Carlo layer is checked against its own degenerate case (all standard deviations zero must reproduce the DCF at the means), the Cholesky factor is checked by reconstruction on all four presets, and the sampled correlation of 20,000 draws lands on the target. The option pricer reproduces the textbook Black-Scholes call (S=K=100, T=1, σ=20%, r=5% → 10.4506).

About this model

Whose work is what: the valuation model is Aswath Damodaran's (NYU Stern) — his Ginzu spreadsheet, including its assumption overrides, failure adjustment, NOL shielding, R&D capitalisation and dilution-adjusted option pricing. I scoped and directed this web rebuild with AI coding agents rather than hand-writing the code, and I did the validation: reading the workbook's formulas and VBA, building the cell-level test harness, and reconciling every discrepancy until the two engines agreed. The finance judgement — which drivers to stress, how wide to set them, whether they should move together, and what the resulting distribution says about the price — is the part I bring to a valuation.

How the DCF works

Revenues grow at the next-year rate, then the years 2–5 CAGR, then converge linearly to the perpetual growth rate over years 6–10. Operating margin converges linearly from next year's margin to the target by the convergence year. Reinvestment is next year's revenue change divided by the sales-to-capital ratio (a one-year lag between investing and growth, overridable). The effective tax rate drifts to the marginal rate over years 6–10; net operating losses shield income until exhausted. Cost of capital converges to a terminal rate (risk-free + mature-market premium, or an override) and the terminal reinvestment rate is g ÷ ROIC. Value of operating assets = PV(FCFF 1–10) + PV(terminal value), adjusted for the probability of failure; equity = that, minus debt and minority interests, plus cash and non-operating assets, minus the value of employee options.

How the Monte Carlo works

Four value drivers are modelled as correlated normals. The correlation matrix is Cholesky-decomposed (L·Lᵀ = R); each trial draws four independent standard normals, multiplies by L, scales by the standard deviations and adds the means. The four sampled values replace the long-term inputs — not the year-1 inputs, which is a mistake the validation caught in an earlier build — and the full DCF is re-run. Percentiles use Excel's PERCENTILE.EXC convention to match the workbook.
